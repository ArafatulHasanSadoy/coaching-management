# Trying out the app — a guide for the coaching owner

Thank you for testing this. It runs entirely on the phone: no internet is
needed for anything below, and nothing leaves the device.

**The most useful thing you can tell us is where it does not match how your
centre actually works.** A feature that works perfectly but is not how you do
things is still wrong.

---

## Before you start

1. Install the APK. Android will warn that it is not from the Play Store —
   that is expected for a test build.
2. Have ready, if you can:
   - your **student register** (Excel or CSV)
   - a **filled money receipt** you currently hand to guardians
   - **last month's routine** as you built it
   - a **past question paper**
   - the **fixed pages you reprint**, like the diary sheet, as PDF

## First run

You will be asked for a **master passphrase** and a **PIN**.

- The passphrase encrypts everything and is the only thing that can open a
  backup on a new phone. **Write it down somewhere other than this phone.**
  Nobody can recover it — not even us.
- The PIN is just for quick unlocking day to day.

Then a four-step setup: your centre's name, the academic session, and which
classes you teach. Classes and subjects come pre-filled from the national
curriculum — tick what applies.

> **Tell us:** did anything in setup not fit your centre? Class names, subject
> names, the way you name batches?

---

## The things worth trying, in order

### 1. Get your students in
**Students → Import (top right).** Choose your register file. The app guesses
which column is which — check its guesses and correct any that are wrong.

> **Tell us:** how many columns did it get right? Did any student fail to
> import, and did the reason make sense?

### 2. Find someone the way you actually do
On the students screen, type a **guardian's phone number**.

> **Tell us:** is this how your front desk looks people up? Is anything missing
> from what you see about a student?

### 3. Take a fee and print the receipt
**Dues → the ⊞ button** raises this month's fees for everyone. Then tap a
student, and collect.

> **Tell us:** compare the printed receipt with the one you hand out today.
> What is missing? What is in the wrong place? Is the wording right?

### 4. Take a register
**Attendance → pick a batch.** Everyone starts marked present; tap only the
ones who are absent.

> **Tell us:** how long did a real class take? Would a teacher use this while
> standing in front of the students?

### 5. Build a routine
**Routine → New routine.**

Tap the **checklist icon** in the top bar first. It asks you five questions —
how many classes and batches, how many teachers, how many rooms, how many
periods in a day, and how often each subject meets — and shows which of them it
already knows the answer to. It will not build much until those are answered,
and it tells you which one is missing rather than producing an empty grid.

The most important one is the last: **Subjects each week**. Each batch has its
own list, because two batches of the same class rarely study the same subjects.
Add or remove a subject for one batch there, set how many periods a week it
gets, and say who teaches it. Tap a subject to change its teacher or drop it
from that batch.

Then use the **sparkle icon** to fill the grid in. It places everything it can
and **leaves the rest blank** — a blank cell means it could not place anything
there without a clash, not that it gave up. Tap any blank cell and it lists
only what actually fits at that hour.

Two views:
- **Build by batch** (the default) — one batch's own week. This is where you
  build and correct.
- **Whole class** — a class's batches merged onto one sheet, each cell marked
  with its batch. This is the one for the notice board. Print follows whichever
  view you are on.

**Copying a week:** with a batch selected, tap the **clipboard icon** to copy
another batch's whole week into it. Subjects are matched by name, so you can
copy Class 9's week onto Class 10. Where the target batch has already named a
teacher for a subject, that teacher is used — not the one being copied from.
Anything that would clash is left blank, and it tells you exactly how many
periods were left out and why.

> **Tell us:** compare what it produces with the routine you built by hand.
> What did it get wrong? If it could not place something, did the explanation
> make sense and was the suggested fix realistic? Is copying between batches
> how you would actually do it?

### 6. Make a question paper
**Questions and papers.** Add a few questions to the bank, create a paper, add
a section, and fill it. Print it.

> **Tell us:** does the printed paper look like what your teachers produce? Is
> the Bangla correct? Is anything about the layout wrong?

### 7. Your own printed forms
**Print centre → Upload a form.** Add your diary page and set which days of the
month you print it. The app will then show it as due.

> **Tell us:** is this how you would want to be reminded?

### 8. Back it up
**Backup → Back up now**, then **Send a copy off this phone**.

> **Please do this before entering any real data you care about, and again at
> the end.**

## What is deliberately not here yet

So you are not looking for them:

- No SMS to guardians, no parent app, no online payment.
- No exams, marks or report cards — question papers are here, results are not.
- No photographing a printed question to import it (OCR), and no OMR scanning.
- Nothing syncs between phones. One phone holds everything, which is why
  backups matter.

## What we most want to hear

1. **Where does it not match how you work?** Wording, order of steps, a field
   you need that is not there, a step you do that the app does not.
2. **Anything that felt slow**, and what you were doing at the time.
3. **Anything that looked wrong on paper** — a receipt, a routine, a question
   paper, an ID card.
4. **Anything you were afraid to press**, and why.

Rough notes are perfectly useful. "The receipt should show the month, not the
date" is worth more than a long report.
